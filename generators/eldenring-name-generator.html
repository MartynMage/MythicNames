About Soulslike Naming

FromSoftware naming has a signature: a plain, almost archaic name followed by a grand title. Godrick the Grafted. Rennala, Queen of the Full Moon. The name alone is often unremarkable — it is the epithet that does the work, and the epithet usually describes a fate rather than an achievement.

The other trick is decay. Titles reference things already lost: the First, the Fell, the Ever-Brilliant, the Severed. Grandeur in the past tense.

The Patterns

  • Name plus title — the title carries the lore
  • Grandeur in decline — titles describe what was, not what is
  • Archaic roots — Old English and Norse fragments
  • Beasts get definite articles — the Fell Omen, the Nameless

MythicNames is an independent fan tool and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or associated with FromSoftware or Bandai Namco. Names are procedurally generated and free to use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these official Elden Ring names?▼
No. This is an independent fan tool with no connection to FromSoftware. It generates original names in the style the games use, rather than reusing characters from them.
What makes a name sound soulslike?▼
The structure more than the sound. A short archaic name followed by a grand title — Name, the Adjective Noun — is the pattern doing most of the work.
Why do the titles sound so mournful?▼
FromSoftware titles usually reference something already lost or corrupted. Grandeur in the past tense is the house style, and it implies a whole history without explaining any of it.
Can I use these for Dark Souls or Bloodborne?▼
Yes. The three share a naming grammar closely enough that names built for one suit the others, and they work for any decayed high-fantasy setting.
