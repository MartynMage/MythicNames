Names for crusaders, hedge knights and sworn orders

About Knight Names

Knightly names draw on real medieval naming — Norman, Germanic and Frankish roots that already sound weighty to a modern ear. The name itself is usually plain; the grandeur comes from the title and the house attached to it.

Naming Traditions

  • Germanic roots — elements like -bert, -ric, -mund and -hard
  • Title first — Ser, Sir or Dame carries more weight than the name
  • Landed vs unlanded — a hedge knight has no estate to be named for

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a good knight name?▼
Real medieval roots. Germanic and Norman elements such as -ric, -bert, -mund and -hard sound authentic because they genuinely were the naming stock of the period.
Should I add a title?▼
Yes — Sir, Ser or Dame does a lot of work. In many settings the title plus a plain given name reads as more authentic than an elaborate invented name.
What is a hedge knight?▼
A knight with no lands and no lord, travelling between tournaments for prize money. Their names are humbler, without a noble estate attached.
How do knightly order names work?▼
Members of a sworn order are often known by the order rather than a family estate, using formal Germanic names and a rank within the chapter.
