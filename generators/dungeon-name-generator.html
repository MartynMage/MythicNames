About Dungeon Names

A dungeon name is a warning label. It should tell the party roughly what kind of trouble is inside and how old the trouble is, in about four words.

Naming Patterns

  • The Adjective Place — The Weeping Catacombs
  • Place of Owner — the previous occupant gets billing
  • Local names — what nearby villagers call it is often better than its real name

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I name a dungeon?▼
Pair a mood adjective with a structure noun. The Screaming Vaults tells a party more in three words than a paragraph of description.
Should the name spoil what is inside?▼
A hint is good, a full reveal is not. The Drowned Halls suggests water hazards without confirming what lives there.
What if the dungeon has two names?▼
That is a great detail. The formal name on old maps and the blunt name locals use can differ, and the gap is a hook in itself.
How do I make a dungeon feel ancient?▼
Reference something the current world has forgotten — a dead dynasty, an abandoned calendar, a god nobody worships now.
