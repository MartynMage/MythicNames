About Wizard Names

Wizard names carry the weight of study. Where a warrior earns a name in one afternoon, a wizard accumulates one over decades — often with a title, a tradition, and at least one epithet awarded by rivals rather than friends.

Naming Traditions

  • Long vowels — names with room to breathe suit scholarly magic
  • Titles matter — Archmage, Magister, Adept signal standing
  • Tradition shapes sound — a hedge witch and an archmage should not sound alike

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a good wizard name?▼
Length and rhythm. Wizard names benefit from more syllables than a fighter would carry — they suggest study, age and accumulated title.
Should a wizard have a title?▼
Titles like Archmage, Magister or Adept do useful work, signalling where the character sits in a magical hierarchy without any exposition.
How do hedge witch names differ?▼
Hedge witches use plain, folksy names drawn from plants and village life. The contrast with a formal archmage name is a good way to signal class and setting.
Do elementalist names follow a pattern?▼
Often they carry a root tied to their element — fire, frost, storm or stone — either in the name itself or in an epithet earned through practice.
