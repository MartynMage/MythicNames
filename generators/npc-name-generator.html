About NPC Names

NPC names have one job: be sayable and memorable the first time. A player will hear it once, across a table, over dice noise. Two syllables and a trade beats an elaborate invented name every time.

Naming Patterns

  • Name plus trade — Bram Cooper, Hal Fletcher
  • Name plus quirk — how the village describes them
  • Keep it short — you will improvise this one under pressure

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I name an NPC quickly?▼
Pair a short given name with an occupational surname. It is instantly pronounceable, tells the players what the NPC does, and takes no thought at the table.
Why do occupational surnames work so well?▼
Because that is where real surnames came from. Smith, Cooper, Fletcher and Baker were jobs first, so they carry an automatic ring of authenticity.
Should every NPC have a full name?▼
No. A shopkeeper the party will meet once can just be Old Meg. Save full names for people who will recur.
How do I make an NPC memorable?▼
Attach one concrete detail to the name — a habit, a grudge, a physical tic. Players remember the detail and the name comes with it.
