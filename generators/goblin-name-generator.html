About Goblin Names

Goblin names are short, harsh and easy to shout across a battlefield. They cluster around hard consonants and clipped vowels, and most goblins earn a second name from a deed, a scar, or whatever they stole most recently.

Naming Traditions

  • One or two syllables — anything longer gets shortened by the tribe
  • Hard sounds — K, G, Z, and harsh clusters like gr, sn, skr
  • Earned epithets — Snik the Gnawer, Grub Tooth-Taker

Frequently Asked Questions

How do goblin names work in D&D?▼
Goblins take a short given name at birth and often earn a descriptive second name from a deed or a physical trait. Tribal identity matters more than family lineage.
What makes a name sound goblin?▼
Hard consonants, clipped vowels and one or two syllables. Sounds like k, g, z and clusters such as gr, sn and skr do most of the work.
Are hobgoblin names different?▼
Yes. Hobgoblins are militaristic and their names are longer and more formal, often paired with a rank or legion rather than a nickname.
Can goblin names be funny?▼
Absolutely — comic goblin names are a long tradition. Pairing a menacing given name with a petty epithet is reliably good at the table.
